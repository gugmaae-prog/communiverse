## Position in one line

Communiverse is pre-launch. Its moat is not yet built. The thesis is that a verified, consented and continuing relationship with each artisan earns across five revenue lines, and that this compounds in a way that listing and booking platforms can't copy cheaply. Today the evidence is the design, the demo and the early supply scouting. There are no signed artisans and no transactions [KF-20, KF-23, KF-24].

## Threats (ADI External Pressure, dimensions inferred)

Disintermediation. Etsy, Airbnb Experiences or AI booking agents could list the same artisans. Mitigation (hypothesis): verification, consent and multi-line earnings make the relationship stickier than a single listing. This is unproven until artisans are retained and earning on several lines.

Synthetic-content flood. Mitigation: in-person verification and provenance records (provenance is planned for the US marketplace beta, Q2 2028, per s15).

Platform dependency. Distribution relies on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube (s11; ambassador audiences [KF-32 to KF-34]).

Thin supply moat today. "A verified supply network rivals would have to rebuild city by city" (s11) is a target, not a current asset [KF-20].

## What makes this defensible later (and how Amaya can check it)

Verified and consented artisans per city, with retention (needs a protocol and a CRM).

Revenue lines per artisan, and revenue per artisan over time (needs the model and transactions).

Cost per signed artisan through ambassadors vs paid acquisition (Foundations scorecard).

A proprietary data asset: verification records, bookings and content rights, owned by Common Folk Group, Inc. (IP assignment is GAP; see 05 Legal & Regulatory).

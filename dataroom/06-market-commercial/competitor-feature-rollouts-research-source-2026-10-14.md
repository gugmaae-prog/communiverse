# Competitor Feature Rollouts: Communiverse Data Room (Competitive Landscape)

Research date: 14 Oct 2026. Public sources only; every figure is cited. "n/d" = not disclosed.

## 1. Artsy (NYC, founded 2009; online art marketplace for galleries, auctions and fairs)

| Date | Rollout / change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 9, 2012 | Public launch. Discovery plus editorial, with inquiry-led sales: buyers contacted galleries directly and Artsy did not transact | artsy-timeline.netlify.app; uschamber.com |
| Oct 2013 | First benefit auction (ICI) | artsy-timeline.netlify.app |
| Mar 2014 | Art-fair tech debuts at The Armory Show; became its digital partner in 2015 | artsy-timeline.netlify.app |
| 2015 | Pivot into commercial auctions: LAMA first, then a Sotheby's online-only sale (announced Sep 8, 2015) | files.artsy.net/sothebys-artsy/press-release |
| Jun 2016 | Live Auction Integration: remote real-time bidding in Christie's, Sotheby's, Phillips and Heritage salerooms | artsy.github.io (Aug 9, 2016); pymnts.com |
| Jul 18, 2017 | $50M Series D at a $275M valuation, aimed at "doubling down" on auctions | techcrunch.com (Jul 18, 2017) |
| 2019–2020 | New CEO Mike Steib. Mobile-first reorg; new app home page, profiles and push settings | artsy.github.io (Sep 29, 2020) |
| 2020 | Buy Now / Make Offer (15% commission). Gallery subscription raised (e.g. $375 to $650/mo), with plans running $200 to $3,000+/mo | theartnewspaper.com (Mar 23, 2020) |
| 2021 | Android app (React Native), "affinity score" recommendation model, shipping/tax integrations (Arta, Avalara) | artsy.github.io (Jan 6, 2022) |
| Ongoing | Free Price Database (auction records for 300k artists) used as a top-of-funnel tool | artsy.net/price-database |

Success metrics reported: first auction had 100% sell-through, and 85% of winning bids came from Artsy collectors (2015). 41 auctions in 2016, on track to quadruple in 2017. ~$20M in sales facilitated per month (2017). Artsy accounted for 1/3 of bids and 9% of sales in the auctions it hosted. Later figures: ~4,000 paying partners, 1.7M registered users, 2.3M monthly uniques, 1M+ app downloads. Financials n/d. Signal: Artsy went from discovery/content to transactions and then to mobile. Monetisation moved from subscriptions to commission on direct checkout. The 2020 price hikes upset small galleries, which shows the risk of charging the supply side heavily.

## 2. Etsy (NYC; creative-goods marketplace, public company)

| Date | Rollout / change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 16, 2018 | Seller transaction fee raised from 3.5% to 5%, now also applied to shipping | sec.gov R10 (FY2020 10-K) |
| 2019 | Reverb acquired. Unified "Etsy Ads" in Q3 2019 | sec.gov FY2020/FY2021 10-K |
| May 4, 2020 | Offsite Ads: Etsy pays for the ads and sellers pay 12–15% only when an ad leads to a sale | sec.gov FY2020 10-K |
| 2020 | Etsy Payments expanded to 45 countries; 92% of GMS ran through it | sec.gov FY2020 10-K |
| Jul 2021 | Acquired Depop and Elo7 ("House of Brands") | sec.gov FY2021 10-K |
| Q3 2021 | Star Seller program defines and rewards seller quality standards | sec.gov FY2021 10-K |
| 2024 | Gift Mode (~5,000 gift ideas) and Etsy Insider, a paid buyer membership (US beta) | investors.etsy.com Q3 2024; help.etsy.com |

Success metrics reported: FY2020: $10.3B GMS, 81.9M active buyers (+76.7%), 4.4M sellers, Q4 take rate 17.1% (+80bps). Offsite Ads opt-out was under 2%, and 9% of H2 GMS was subject to the fee. Habitual buyers +157% and repeat buyers +97% (Q4 2020). FY2021: 96.3M buyers and 7.5M sellers across all marketplaces; Offsite Ads revenue offset ~33% of performance marketing spend. FY2024: $12.587B GMS (−4.4%), 95.5M buyers, take rate 22.3%. FY2025: $11.917B GMS (−5.3%), 93.5M buyers, take rate 24.2%, with the app at 45% of GMS. (investors.etsy.com 8-Ks; 10-Ks) Signal: Etsy grew revenue mainly by raising take rate (fees, ads, payments) while GMS shrank after 2021. Retention is measured with buyer cohorts (habitual and repeat buyers). Quality is enforced through seller badges, and the paid buyer membership is a loyalty play.

## 3. Saatchi Art (Los Angeles; curated marketplace for emerging artists, owned by Leaf Group)

2010: commercial launch. Early 2011: online sales for artists begin. Jan 2014: renamed from Saatchi Online to Saatchi Art (crunchbase.com/organization/saatchiart).

Current model: artists apply with a 3-work portfolio and verify their identity. Curators review uploads daily. Art Advisors sell to collectors in 140+ countries, and a trade team sources work for commercial clients. "Rising Stars" and digital exhibitions provide editorial. Commission is 40% on originals vs ~50% at most galleries (saatchiart.com/whysell).

Metrics: n/d publicly.

Signal: this is the closest analogue to Plug's "verified emerging artists". Its edge comes from curation plus a human advisory layer, not from the software.

## 4. Singulart (Paris, 2017; online gallery for established and emerging artists)

2017: launch. 2018: 1,500 artists across 80 countries. 2020: 10,000 artists, 100 staff, €10M Series A (Ventech). Nov 19, 2021: €60M Series B (Vitruvian) and launch of balthasart for up-and-coming artists and first-time collectors. Later: physical galleries in Stuttgart and Berlin, a Trade offering with 5* hotels and interior designers, iconic-artist works (Picasso, Dalí), and a service matching artists with gallerists (singulart.com/en/about; singulart.com/blog 2021/11/19).

Metrics: 20,000+ collectors (2021). Today 9,000 artists from 165 countries and 110K artworks sold.

Signal: it expanded into B2B trade and hospitality, went phygital with physical galleries, and split off a sub-brand for entry-level buyers.

## 5. Artfinder (UK; independent-artist marketplace, B Corp)

Crowdcube round: £834,056 from 1,320 investors, many of them artists and customers. Wellington Partners had invested £5m to date. Funds go to faster artist payouts, better buyer and seller UX, and customer acquisition (artfinder.com/blog/post/artfinder-crowdfunding).

Signal: community ownership and B Corp status act as a moat, and it competes on artist-payout speed.

## 6. Masterworks (NYC; fractional investment in blue-chip art)

Shares of single-artwork Series LLCs sell at $20 under SEC Reg A, with a secondary trading market. Fees are 1.5%/yr plus 20% of profit. Oct 5, 2021: $110M Series A at a $1B+ valuation (Left Lane); the company was bootstrapped and profitable before that (techcrunch.com 2021/10/05; globenewswire). Membership is now members-only, by sponsorship (masterworks.com).

Metrics: 940k+ members, 419 works and ~$1B AUM (Jun 2024, benzinga). 530+ works acquired, 32 sold and $77M+ distributed (masterworks.com).

Signal: art as an asset class. Exclusive, invite-only membership drives demand, which is relevant to Communiverse's "club" and ambassador model.

## 7. MENA / Dubai players

Art Dubai Digital (2022): the fair's first digital-art section, with Bybit as lead partner and 17 presentations; it has since moved toward AI, immersive and phygital work (alarabiya.net 2022/03/17; artdubai.ae blog 2024).

Art Smiley (Dubai-founded): 3,500+ artists, selling physical and digital art plus NFTs (gulf-times.com).

POPC (Dubai): online marketplace for local and global artists (thenationalnews.com 2023/05/15).

Signal: the local market is fragmented and listing-led. No regional player combines verified artists, community circles and bookable artist sessions.

## Signals for Communiverse: potential pivots and feature priorities

Start with discovery and community, then move to transactions. Artsy started as discovery/inquiry and only later added checkout and auctions. Launch Plug with direct checkout from day one, but measure the community layer (circles, ambassadors) as the acquisition engine.

Keep supply-side fees low and earn from success-based fees and buyer services. Artsy's subscription hikes drew backlash. Etsy's success-based Offsite Ads had under 2% opt-out. Use commission plus success-based promotion rather than artist subscriptions.

Make verification a visible product. Saatchi curation and Etsy's Star Seller show this works. "Verified" Plug badges with clear standards are a defensibility point.

Add a human advisory / trade layer. Saatchi's advisors and Singulart's Trade arm show it. In Dubai, hotels, developers and interior designers are a natural B2B channel to pilot by mid-2027.

Treat artist sessions and experiences as the differentiator. No competitor reviewed sells bookable time with artists. Track sessions booked, rebook rate and session-to-artwork conversion.

Use a club and membership structure. Masterworks (sponsor-only) and Etsy Insider (paid membership) show it can work. An ambassador-referral, tiered membership fits Communiverse.

Report investor-grade KPIs from launch, the way Etsy does: GMV, active buyers, repeat and habitual buyers, take rate, active verified artists, sell-through and app share of GMV.

Watch-outs: auctions and fractional investing are capital- and regulation-heavy (Masterworks' SEC Reg A route), so keep them out of the 12–18 month roadmap. Phygital pop-ups (Singulart galleries, Art Dubai Digital) are a cheaper lever.

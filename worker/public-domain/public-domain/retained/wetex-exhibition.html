Compass of the Sea

Acrylic on four canvases · 60 × 60 cm · 2026

Waves meet at a shared centre, then turn outward. Moon phases hold the rhythm of return and renewal.

Read the artwork statement

Compass of the Sea reflects the ocean’s instinct to leave and come back. Waves enter from the four cardinal directions, north, south, east and west, moving towards a shared centre before turning outward once more. Arranged across four canvases, their repeated motion transforms the composition into a continuous cycle rather than a fixed scene.

The phases of the moon form the quiet centre of the work, acknowledging its gravitational influence on water and the changing tides. As the moon waxes, wanes and returns, the sea follows its rhythm, advancing, withdrawing and beginning again.

The work considers return not as a repetition of what has already happened, but as renewal. Like water returning to the shore, each arrival carries the memory of its journey while making space for another beginning.

Visual Noise · Sky

Wisps of white cross a blue sky, interrupted by a pale folded rectangle at the centre.

Read the series statement

Through visual mimicry and photorealism, Visual Noise brings attention to peripheral objects we often overlook. The works highlight dissonance and contradiction, telling a story of observation and awareness.

By reflecting the contradictions within objects and the imposition of foreign elements, the series considers personal dilemmas and cultural contradictions. It gives visual form to the obscuring and obstruction of progress.

Meet the artist↗

Shared Memories

Watercolor collage with dried petals · 21 × 21 cm

Watercolor fragments and dried petals gather into a collage of brief encounters, shared smiles and memories that continue to bloom.

Kolaheng Papel Series - IV

Read the artist’s statement

In my work, I often treat colors and shapes as fragments of memories—little pieces of moments that have stayed with me. While my previous works have been reflections of my own experiences, Shared Memories extends beyond the personal. It holds traces of the people I have met, the strangers I have exchanged smiles with, and the fleeting moments of connection that quietly become part of us.

The petals symbolize how even the smallest gestures and simplest encounters can leave a lasting impression. Like flowers slowly blooming, these little memories gather and unfold within my heart, reminding me that not all meaningful moments need to be grand or familiar. Sometimes, a shared smile is enough to make something bloom.

A painting can make us spend time with something we usually pass by. These works give us a way into conversations about water, wildlife and the things we choose to keep.

Water is part of our lives

Chandni’s tides and Sophiko’s sea life invite us to spend time with the ocean. What do we notice in water, and what would we miss if it changed?

What would you care for longer?

A place, an object, a living thing. Choose a work, talk about what you see, and take that question with you.

Simple ways to make the exhibition less wasteful

For this show, we propose borrowing display furniture, reusing frames and packing materials, and offering a QR guide alongside a few large-print copies. Use the existing lights and screen where suitable, and switch them off when the space closes.

These are choices for the exhibition team to confirm. The works’ subjects do not, on their own, tell us their environmental footprint.
